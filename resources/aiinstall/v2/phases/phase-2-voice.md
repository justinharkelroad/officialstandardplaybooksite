# Phase 2. Brand voice

Outcome: you can draft communication that sounds recognizably like the owner across team, client, and prospect contexts. The owner does not describe their voice from memory. You study what they actually wrote, and they grade the test.

## What to do

1. Read every writing sample in the folder. If you find fewer than five, say so and ask where the rest are before continuing.
2. Extract the voice:
   - Sentence length and rhythm.
   - Phrases they repeat.
   - How they open and close messages.
   - How tone shifts between team, clients, and prospects.
   - Words, phrases, and tones they clearly never use.
3. Write a short test email to their team about a normal week, in their voice.
4. The owner grades it line by line. For every correction, state the reusable voice rule behind it. Do not save anything yet.
5. When the owner says the test draft is approved, save `brand-voice.md` with these sections: voice rules, tone by audience (team / clients / prospects), phrases I actually use, and the never-say list.
6. Show the final file.

## Test

Using `brand-voice.md`, draft two short messages about the same normal agency update: one to the team and one to a current client. The facts stay the same. The tone changes by audience. Do not send either message.

## Pass condition

- `brand-voice.md` exists.
- Every rule is supported by the owner's real samples.
- It distinguishes team, client, and prospect tone.
- It includes a never-say list.
- The owner approved at least one real test draft.

## If something goes wrong

- You cannot find samples: ask the owner to confirm the samples are inside the connected folder, or to give their exact location.
- The output reads flat or generic: ask for more conversational samples. Quick messages and transcripts teach voice better than polished copy.
