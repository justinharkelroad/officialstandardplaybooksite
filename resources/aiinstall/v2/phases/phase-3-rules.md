# Phase 3. Working preferences and safety rules

Outcome: you know how to support the owner daily and weekly, how to format work, how to challenge weak thinking, and where you must stop and wait for approval. This file is the rulebook for the relationship.

## What to do

1. Read `working-preferences-STARTER.md`. It is the shape of the file. Do not rename or delete it.
2. Interview the owner briefly, one question at a time. The owner may be speaking. Cover:
   - What they want help with every day and every week.
   - Their biggest time drains.
   - How they want outputs delivered: ready to use, or drafts they edit.
   - How to push back when you think they are wrong.
3. Write `working-preferences.md`. Add the four safety rules exactly as written below, under the heading "The four safety rules (non-negotiable, apply to every output)". Do not reword them.

   1. **Nothing sends without my sign-off.** No email, text, or post goes to staff, clients, or prospects until I approve it.
   2. **Never invent numbers.** Real data only. If you do not have the number, say so and ask.
   3. **Never touch pricing, commission, or comp.** Those are my decisions alone.
   4. **Never confirm coverage, quote a rate, or bind anything in a draft.** Client-facing drafts can cover service, options, and next steps. Coverage confirmations come from me or my licensed team through carrier systems, never from a drafted message.

4. Add a section "Approvals for anything that acts on its own". Ask the owner, one at a time, how each of these should be handled by any scheduled task or agent working for them: sending a message, paying or moving money, signing a document, deleting anything. Record the answer for each. The default, if the owner is unsure, is "always ask me first".
5. Offer the optional operating rules below. Add them only if the owner approves the wording:

   > Every day, help me identify the three things that matter most, prepare my team communication, follow up on commitments, and determine where I am off pace. When you create a plan, include the owner, deadline, next milestone, and number that determines whether it worked. When information is missing, ask instead of inventing it.

6. Show the file and ask what to correct. Save after approval.

## Test

Using `working-preferences.md`, give a short daily operating brief: the three things that matter most, one team communication to prepare, one commitment that needs follow-up, and where the owner may be off pace. If the files do not contain enough current information, say exactly what is missing instead of making it up.

## Pass condition

- `working-preferences.md` exists.
- No brackets or placeholder text remain in the customized sections.
- All four safety rules appear exactly as written above.
- The approvals section has an answer for sending, paying, signing, and deleting.
- The test respects the owner's output style and approval boundaries.
